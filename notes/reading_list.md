# Literature review: constraint, maintenance, selection and identity loss

Reviewed 2026-10-03, after the initial data tests. This is a targeted primary-literature screen, not a systematic review or proof of novelty. Several publisher pages were intermittently inaccessible; PubMed/PMC records and indexed publisher abstracts were used where indicated. No evidence below validates the proposed model as a whole.

## Novelty assessment

Individual ingredients substantially predate this project. The strongest candidate contribution is a **tested separation of functional constraint mediated by maintenance/restoration from constraint mediated by cell or clone representation**, with independent functional validation. Combining mechanisms in software is insufficient. The current experiments do not establish an independently predictive constraint score, distinguish the mechanisms on real data, or validate identity loss.

The joint function-weighted protection/selection/identity framework remains a candidate synthesis. This search has not established that nobody has built it before. Two particularly close additions to the original design doc are Fu's fidelity inference and EPI-Clone below.

## Biological evidence and its limits

**Maintenance and restoration have direct experimental support.** [Ming et al., 2020, *Kinetics and mechanisms of mitotic inheritance of DNA methylation and their roles in aging-associated methylome deterioration*, Cell Research 30:980–996](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41422-020-0359-9) used single-molecule kinetics and perturbations to separate rapid replication-coupled maintenance from delayed maintenance that can restore methylation. Some sequence/context classes maintained methylation less efficiently. This supports distinct generation/recovery timescales and context covariates. It does not establish that a scalar functional-importance score determines either rate, or that the fitted return rate in an aging array is DNMT1 repair. The article has a 2021 author correction; consult the updated version.

**Fidelity varies by genomic context.** [Ushijima et al., 2003, *Fidelity of the Methylation Pattern and Its Variation in the Genome*, Genome Research 13:868–874](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/430912/) examined clonal human mammary epithelial populations and found differing fidelity across methylation states and genomic regions, including protected unmethylated promoter islands. This supports heterogeneous rates, but comparison of a handful of loci in one lineage is not evidence for genome-wide allocation according to essentiality. Genomic context is a competing explanation for a functional mask.

**Sequence fidelity is a serious alternative explanation.** [López-Moyado et al., 2026, *Flanking DNA sequences determine DNA methylation maintenance in proliferation, cancer and aging*, bioRxiv v1](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.04.09.717557v1) reports a conserved flanking-hexamer ranking related to DNMT1–UHRF1 maintenance and replication-dependent loss. It is a preprint, not established peer-reviewed consensus. The 225-motif table was obtained from the supplement and mapped to probe flanks, averaging both strand orientations. Any claim that externally weighted sites are protected must be tested after sequence matching, especially at proliferative, late-replicating loci.

**Stable loci are useful annotations, not a functional ground truth.** [Basrai et al., 2026, *Blood-based epigenetic instability linked to human aging and disease*, Nature Communications 17:2754](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-69430-z) identifies 31,744 normally unmethylated ESLs, relates instability to aging/disease, and studies persistent methylation in leukemia. ESLs were chosen for low variability and refined across blood cell types; testing their stability in the derivation cohort would be circular. Cancer clone persistence and expression associations motivate validation but do not show that methylation deviations caused selection. The reported mortality association is not uniformly independent of age/sex; the adjusted Framingham mortality result was not significant. Avoid treating every disease association as validated mortality prediction.

**Methylation can causally preserve lineage identity at particular loci.** [Dhawan et al., 2011, *Pancreatic β cell identity is maintained by DNA methylation-mediated repression of Arx*, Developmental Cell 20:419–429](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21497756/) found β-to-α conversion after β-cell Dnmt1 deficiency, alongside Arx derepression. This is stronger than a methylation–expression correlation and supports a lineage-specific identity score. A large maintenance perturbation in pancreas does not establish normal aging in blood, a universal genomic mask, or a scalar first-passage boundary. Arx is a mechanistic validation example, not an annotation to transfer indiscriminately to blood.

**Selection requires lineage or representation information.** [*Clonal tracing with somatic epimutations reveals dynamics of blood ageing*, Nature 643:478–487 (2025), DOI 10.1038/s41586-025-09041-8](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-09041-8) introduces EPI-Clone and distinguishes methylation features of differentiation from stochastic lineage barcodes. It relates age-associated functional output to expanded clones. This provides a more appropriate modality for M3 than an untracked snapshot. However, CpGs used to reconstruct a clone cannot also serve as independent proof that functionally important methylation errors drive its expansion. Reserve separate identity/error loci and independent lineage validation.

**Cell-level aging measurements remain sparse and lineage dependent.** [Bonder et al., 2024, *scEpiAge: an age predictor highlighting single-cell ageing heterogeneity in mouse blood*, Nature Communications 15:7567](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-024-51833-5) provides paired methylomes/transcriptomes and documents sparse coverage and heterogeneous epigenetic age. It motivates the single-cell observation model and RNA validation. It is not repeated measurement of the same cells; chronological ages and QC counts also differ from the later Polycomb reanalysis, so those sample universes must not be conflated.

[Issa's 2014 review, *Aging and epigenetic drift: a vicious cycle*, JCI 124:24–29](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24382386/) already connects drift, epigenetic mosaicism and impaired stem-cell plasticity. This is conceptual prior art, not direct experimental identification of the proposed hazard exp(gamma D).

## Mechanism-specific validation priorities

| Mechanism / claim | Informative experiment | Necessary negative control |
|---|---|---|
| q reduces error generation | Parent–daughter/hairpin or maintenance-kinetics assay after replication, using frozen external q. | Match sequence, density, chromatin, replication timing and initial state; measure conversion errors. |
| q increases restoration | Perturb the methylation state, then measure recovery before substantial differential survival. | Control division rate, survival, target equilibrium and direct drug effects. |
| q drives selection | Track clones and methylation before/after lineage persistence or dropout. | Independent lineage barcodes; compare immediate errors with survivor errors; include clone output and death/censoring. |
| Global maintenance state | Replicate low-rank residual structure and tail predictions across animals and lineages. | Batch, coverage, proliferation and cell-type adjustment; one-state and continuous alternatives. |
| Weighted distance predicts identity loss | Train reference/weights on separate data; test on paired RNA and a functional assay in held-out donors. | Unweighted distance, matched random masks, prespecified thresholds and direction-specific alternative distances. |

The current code supplies model components and numerical checks for these questions. None of the functional claims is supported merely by the current simulation or aggregate fits. External ESL/sequence/context features and exploratory F2/F8 checks are now available; the next scientific gate is held-out F1 with independent essentiality/identity weights and matching sensitivity; a failed gate should be reported rather than bypassed with latent-state flexibility.

### Additional validation data: longitudinal fibroblast multi-omics

[A multi-omics longitudinal aging dataset in primary human fibroblasts with mitochondrial perturbations](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-022-01852-y), Scientific Data (2022), DOI 10.1038/s41597-022-01852-y. Provides methylation (GSE179847), RNA (GSE179848), culture proliferation and additional physiological measurements across healthy and SURF1 donor cultures. This makes it useful for testing whether methylation distance predicts an independently measured cellular outcome beyond culture duration. The current growth pilot uses endpoint doubling time; RNA remains unanalyzed. Replicative senescence/proliferation slowing is not a predefined loss of fibroblast identity, and common culture-time/treatment effects require held-out covariate comparison.

The original [GSE73115 blood publication](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5557932/) identifies 43 twin pairs. Family-based holdout is required before interpreting person-held-out performance as independent-family generalization. GEO metadata does not disclose the mapping; numbering must not be used to invent pairs.

### Mechanism tests with existing interventions and kinetics

[Busto-Moner et al. (2020)](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371/journal.pcbi.1007195), *Stochastic modeling reveals kinetic heterogeneity in post-replication DNA methylation*. Supplies observed nascent-strand counts and distinguishes maintenance timescales from plateau methylation. Constant hazards and correlated neighboring kinetics predate this project. The new held-out count analysis does not beat a pooled constant in its covered systematic subset; it should not be cited as validation of the novel functional-constraint hypothesis.

[A multivalent signal-transducing model for DNMT1-mediated maintenance](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE145698): public mouse ES-cell WT/mutant rescue counts provide intervention evidence for maintenance machinery and domain interactions. The simple independent-effect genotype transfer fails W465A/W796A. This challenges the project's simplest perturbation mapping while leaving broader CTMC modeling open; generic maintenance plausibility is established prior biology, not project novelty.

[EPI-Clone (2025)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-09041-8) supplies independent genetic clone labels plus methylation-sensitive amplification and proteins. Extra static-locus predictability from clone labels is expected prior evidence, not novel protection/selection identification. Different recipient compositions and within-clone change must be distinguished, and static barcode loci must not be mislabeled as functional identity errors.


## Functional recovery and experiment selection (2026-10-03)

Yang et al. (2014), [Gene Body Methylation can alter Gene Expression and is a Therapeutic Target in Cancer](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4224113/), supplies HCT116 pulse/washout and DNMT knockout comparisons. Recovery is not synonymous with DNMT1 maintenance: the published findings implicate DNMT3B in rebound. Our essentiality-stratified reanalysis remains confounded and under-supported after expression QC. Its contribution is candidate/assay selection, not a new demonstration of functional constraint.

The [BAGEL reference lists](https://github.com/hart-lab/bagel/tree/53388adbb4fb0931e5c9dda135502be19e4555f0) provide independent gene-fitness categories. [Kim and Hart (2021)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13073-020-00809-3) describes the associated classifier. Gene knockout essentiality does not establish functional necessity of a mapped CpG or gene-body region.

[Liu et al. (2016), Editing DNA Methylation in the Mammalian Genome](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27662091/), and [Morita et al. (2016)](https://www.nature.com/articles/nbt.3658) establish feasibility of targeted methylation editing at particular loci. They support an experimental tool, not editability or function of our four candidate regions. The local-function gate and prospective recovery design are in [experiment_brief.md](experiment_brief.md). Catalytically inactive occupancy controls, editor clearance, and separation of 5mC from 5hmC are required for interpreting a TET-based test.


## Broader functional and recovery data identified during cost optimization

[Tejedor et al. (2023), Clinical Epigenetics](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13148-023-01546-1) provides local active/dead-editor and transcriptional-activation comparisons across 56 promoter targets in two colorectal lines. These can replace knockout essentiality with intervention-based functional evidence, subject to catalytic specificity, edit success and screen selection. It has no recovery trajectories.

[Masalmeh et al. (2021), Nature Communications](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-020-20716-w) supplies human RRBS pulse/washout and enzyme comparisons, with H3K36me3 as an established recruitment mechanism. Variable recovery slopes do not generally rule out selection; use chromatin context as a competing explanation for functional scores.

[Ginno et al. (2020), Nature Communications](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-020-16354-x) already models site-specific methylation/demethylation kinetics and identifiability under enzyme deletion. Rate heterogeneity and context effects alone are not our novelty. Mouse enzyme dynamics do not directly validate human local functional importance. [Achinger-Kawecka et al. (2024), Nature Structural & Molecular Biology](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41594-023-01181-7) offers a separate breast-cancer withdrawal setting with expression/chromatin readouts; recovery-associated transcription is established biology, not our novel mechanism.
